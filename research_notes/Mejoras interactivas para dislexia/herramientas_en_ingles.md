# Digital Tools in English for Children Aged 6–9 With Dyslexia or Reading Difficulties

Research date: 2026-10-14. Budget: about 16 tool calls, many tools covered. Most mechanics come from vendor or school pages. Independent evidence is thin for most tools except Lexia Core5 and Amira. Items marked "unverified" come from the researcher's background knowledge, not a fetched source. Treat them as hypotheses.

## Q1. Skills trained and concrete activities, per tool

### Takeaway
Most products follow the same loop: a short explicit demo or strategy video, then practice games on one phonics or phonological skill, then a decodable or leveled text. AI tutors (Amira, Ello, Google Read Along, Microsoft Reading Coach) add a final step where the child reads aloud and speech recognition gives feedback. For most products the sourced detail stops at the skill level. Exact game mechanics are rarely documented publicly.

### Cited Findings
**Lexia Core5 Reading (Pre-K–5)**
- Six strands: phonological awareness, phonics, vocabulary, structural analysis, automaticity/fluency and comprehension. Students choose their own path and pace. Runs on PC, Mac, Chromebook, iPad and Android tablets — [Lexia llms-full.txt](https://www.lexialearning.com/llms-full.txt)
- A unit takes about 5–7 minutes — [Bend-La Pine Schools tech blog](https://blogs.bend.k12.or.us/instructionaltechnology/lexia-reading/)
- Offline teacher resources: Lexia Lessons (scripted explicit lessons) and Lexia Skill Builders (practice worksheets) for small groups or individuals — [Lexia 2025 "With What Time" infographic](https://lexialearning.com/user_area/content_media/processed/2025-Q1-C5-With-What-Time-Infographic-v1-2-WEB.pdf)
- Core5 itself has no speech recognition. Lexia's speech recognition is in a different product, Lexia English Language Development — [Lexia llms-full.txt](https://www.lexialearning.com/llms-full.txt)

**Nessy Reading & Spelling (UK origin, ages 5+)**
- 100 lessons across 10 islands. Each lesson combines strategy videos, games and printable worksheets — [schoolreadinglist.co.uk on Nessy](https://schoolreadinglist.co.uk/resources/nessy/)
- Sold in a Dyslexia Home Education Pack with the Dyslexia Quest screener, Writing Beach (writing and grammar) and Nessy Fingers (touch typing) — [Nessy Dyslexia Home Pack](https://nessy.com/en-us/shop/home-products/the-dyslexia-home-education-pack/)
- Nessy Fingers uses the Reading & Spelling word lists for typing practice, so typing and spelling are trained together. Custom spelling lists are supported — [Nessy Spell & Type pack](https://www.nessy.com/en-us/product/spell-and-type-pack-home)
- One review says Nessy Fingers introduces keys alphabetically and is organized into five islands — [SPELD NSW typing programs](https://www.speldnsw.org.au/parents-hub/typing-programs/)

**Reading Eggs and Fast Phonics (3P Learning)**
- Reading Eggs has 120 structured lessons covering phonemic awareness, phonics, high-frequency words, vocabulary, fluency and comprehension. Each lesson opens with a demonstration and ends with a real book where the student applies the skill — [Reading Eggs program overview](https://readingeggs.com/schools/program-overview/)
- Fast Phonics (K–Grade 5) is a synthetic phonics program covering letter-sound recognition, blending and spelling — [Sophie Online School family guide](https://sophie.onlineschool.ca/books/learning-commons-for-families/page/reading-eggs-fast-phonics-reading-eggspress/export/html)

**Amira Learning (HMH, K–5)**
- Listens to the child read aloud, analyzes oral reading fluency in real time, and gives instant feedback and instructional guidance. Descends from Carnegie Mellon's Project LISTEN Reading Tutor (1997) — [FCRR summary](https://fcrr.org/node/7576); [TechLearning](https://www.techlearning.com/news/houghton-mifflin-harcourt-partners-with-ai-powered-reading-startup)
- HMH markets "dozens of micro-interventions" rooted in the science of reading. They are not described publicly in detail — [HMH Amira](https://www.hmhco.com/amira-access)
- Tutoring works in English and Spanish. "Amira Instruct" builds differentiated lesson plans for teachers — [Amira research page](https://amiralearning.com/research)

**Ello (Pre-K–3)**
- The AI reading coach listens with patent-pending speech recognition, follows the child's reading and helps with hard words. Its "Adaptive Learn" engine follows a science-of-reading phonics scope and sequence — [Ello app listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1536720182); [Yahoo Finance press release](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ello-leverages-genai-launch-largest-131500632.html)
- Library of 700+ exclusive decodable e-books matched to level (some pages say 800+). An optional physical subscription mails 5 books a month — [Ello access page](https://www.ello.com/access)
- A parent testimonial says the child taps a word to get help from the on-screen elephant (an anecdote) — [CO/AI tool listing](https://getcoai.com/tool-detail/ello/164021/)

**Microsoft Reading Coach / Reading Progress / Immersive Reader**
- After the child reads a passage aloud, Reading Coach picks the 5 most challenging words. For each one the child can listen, see it split into syllables, and record themselves until the word turns green — [LCPS Inclusive Design blog](https://blogs.lcps.org/inclusivedesign/tag/reading-coach); [Glow Scotland guide](https://blogs.glowscotland.org.uk/fa/digilearnfalk/2025/03/04/microsoft-reading-coach-get-started/)
- In Reading Progress, the teacher assigns a passage, the student records it, and the software auto-detects errors and computes correct words per minute and accuracy — [Microsoft Education blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/education/blog/2023/02/enhance-literacy-development-with-reading-progress-and-coach/)

**Google Read Along (formerly Bolo)**
- The assistant "Diya" listens while the child reads, flags mistakes and hesitations, corrects errors and congratulates successes. The child can ask for help with any word — [Voicebot.ai](https://voicebot.ai/2020/05/08/google-launches-new-voice-assistant-and-app-to-teach-kids-to-read/); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/google-launches-a-new-read-along-app-to-help-you-with-homeschooling)

**Amplio**
- Amplio Reading Intervention is a digitized structured-literacy dyslexia curriculum with two versions: MTA for English and **Esperanza for Spanish**. It is teacher-delivered to small groups or one-to-one, and Amplio also offers remote interventionists. I found no evidence of child-facing speech recognition — [Amplio home](https://ampliolearning.com/); [Dealroom profile](https://dealroom.co/companies/amplio/)

**Dyslexia Gold (UK)**
- Engaging Eyes is an eye-tracking and convergence game played about 10 minutes a day. Fluency Builder trains phonological awareness, such as hearing the sounds in g-oa-t. Spelling Tutor is a third module. The vendor positions it as a complement to phonics teaching for ages 5–12 — [Dyslexia Action Shop parent bundle](https://dyslexiaactionshop.co.uk/dyslexia-gold-bundle-parents.html); [The Dyslexia Shop](https://thedyslexiashop.co.uk/pages/dyslexia-gold-improve-your-childs-reading-and-spelling)

**Learning Ally**
- Human-narrated audiobooks (50,000+ titles, volunteer-read) with highlighted text synchronized to the narration. Highlighting speed, reading rate, and text and background colors are adjustable. Users must have a qualifying print disability, such as dyslexia — [Reading Rockets](https://readingrockets.org/resources/literacy-apps/learning-ally-audiobook-reader); [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/learning-ally-audio); [CEC partner page](https://2finfo.exceptionalchildren.org/partner-resources/products/learning-ally)

**Teach Your Monster to Read**
- A phonics adventure game in which the child "teaches" a monster they designed. It follows Phases 2–5 of the UK Letters and Sounds framework and was developed with Roehampton University academics — [Educational App Store](https://www.educationalappstore.com/app/teach-your-monster-to-read)
- Common Sense Education praises the characters but notes little game variety and some confusing gameplay — [Common Sense Education](https://www.commonsense.org/education/app/teach-your-monster-to-read)

**Starfall, Khan Academy Kids, Epic, Hooked on Phonics**
- Starfall: a web and mobile phonics app with listening, watching and interactive play, targeting K–2 — [Educators Technology](https://www.educatorstechnology.com/2023/12/best-learn-to-read-apps.html)
- Khan Academy Kids: free, with no paid tier. Includes a phonics curriculum and hundreds of "Read to Me" books with word highlighting. English-focused, with limited Spanish — [Snappit blog](https://www.snappit.app/blog/best-reading-apps-for-kids); [Bark blog](https://www.bark.us/blog/best-reading-apps-for-kids/)
- Epic: a library of 40,000+ books, audiobooks and videos, with read-to-me books and leveled readers for ages 12 and under — [Bark blog](https://www.bark.us/blog/best-reading-apps-for-kids/); [Shipley School](https://blogs.shipleyschool.org/colleague-notes/reading-apps/)
- Hooked on Phonics: ages 3–7, short animated lessons with music, plus a library of books rated by difficulty — [Bark blog](https://www.bark.us/blog/best-reading-apps-for-kids/)

### Inferences
- Activity patterns that can be reimplemented, inferred from the sources: (1) demo or strategy video, then (2) a one-skill practice game, then (3) a decodable book that uses only the taught graphemes (Reading Eggs, Ello). Another pattern: (4) a read-aloud passage, then (5) automatic choice of 5 problem words, then (6) per-word practice with listening, syllable splitting and re-recording until correct (Microsoft Reading Coach). This second loop is the most precisely documented and the easiest to copy.
- Unverified (background knowledge, not fetched): Nessy lessons are said to use mnemonic "strategy" cartoons for spelling rules and games such as sound-matching and word-building. Lexia Core5 activities are said to include sound blending/segmenting with tiles and timed word recognition ("automaticity"). Teach Your Monster to Read is said to use letter-sound matching mini-games and sound-blending into words. Starfall is said to use letter and word animations. Check these before relying on them.

### Gaps
- I found no published activity-level specs (exact game mechanics) for Lexia Core5, Nessy, Reading Eggs, Hooked on Phonics, Starfall or Amira's micro-interventions.
- I didn't research Beanstack, Readability, "Ava"-branded AI coaches or Nessy's Hairy Letters for lack of budget.

## Q2. Adaptive difficulty and error handling

### Takeaway
The common pattern is a placement test at first login, branching up or down from grade level, mastery checks before advancing, and escalating scaffolds after errors. Lexia is the clearest example: standard activity, then guided practice, then direct instruction on the specific error type, with offline lessons for the teacher. Placement tests are run without supports, so they can under-place a child who is still learning the interface.

### Cited Findings
- Lexia Core5 auto-placement: placement activities at first login start students at grade-level skills and branch them up or down — [PVUSD teacher training guide](https://freedom.pvusd.net/documents/Teachers/Teacher-Training-guide.pdf)
- Scaffolding, feedback and language supports are switched off during Lexia placement so it measures independent proficiency — [Lexia Community FAQ](https://community.lexialearning.com/core5-faqs-85/why-isn-t-language-support-available-in-core5-auto-placement-690)
- Teachers can change Lexia placement manually. Lexia advises checking it against other data — [PVUSD guide](https://freedom.pvusd.net/documents/Teachers/Teacher-Training-guide.pdf)
- Lexia Core5 "scaffold[s] students if they struggle, providing guided practice or even direct instruction on the specific error type," and advances them as they show proficiency. Skill checks verify mastery before a student moves to the next level — [Lexia Core5 overview brochure](https://www.lexialearning.com/resources/guides/lexia-core5-reading-overview-brochure); [Lexia infographic](https://lexialearning.com/user_area/content_media/processed/2025-Q1-C5-With-What-Time-Infographic-v1-2-WEB.pdf)
- The Lexia three-step adaptive model is mentioned without naming the steps — [Lexia llms-full.txt](https://www.lexialearning.com/llms-full.txt)
- When a child makes a mistake, Lexia gives corrective feedback and re-teaches unmastered skills — [Bend-La Pine blog](https://blogs.bend.k12.or.us/instructionaltechnology/lexia-reading/)
- Reading Eggs placement: up to 60 questions on first login, ending automatically after 3 incorrect answers. First attempts can under-place children who are still learning the interface, so a retake is recommended. Teachers can reset the test — [Sophie Online School guide](https://sophie.onlineschool.ca/books/learning-commons-for-families/page/reading-eggs-fast-phonics-reading-eggspress/export/html)
- Nessy: a placement game (Nessy Challenge / Challenge Placement) sets 5 lessons as targets, then the child is reassessed and new targets are set. No adult help is allowed, or the child will be placed too high. Parents can place the child by hand to avoid frustration — [Scargill school guide / schoolreadinglist.co.uk](https://schoolreadinglist.co.uk/resources/nessy/)
- Ello auto-loads books at the child's level and adjusts instruction to progress — [Ello app listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1536720182)

### Inferences
- A design worth copying: placement without supports, then a fixed set of a few target lessons, then reassessment (Nessy), plus a manual teacher/parent override. Early stopping after N errors (Reading Eggs uses 3) limits frustration during testing.
- Error escalation: retry, then a hint/guided step, then a full explicit re-teach, then flag the teacher with an offline lesson (Lexia). This tiered model is the most transferable.

### Gaps
- No public source gave exact mastery thresholds (e.g., % correct) or the number of errors that triggers each Lexia scaffold step.

## Q3. Speech recognition for oral reading and child-facing feedback

### Takeaway
Four tool families use child speech recognition: Amira (real-time ORF scoring plus tutoring), Ello (word-by-word reading buddy with gentle corrections), Google Read Along (Diya, on-device and offline), and Microsoft Reading Coach / Reading Progress (WCPM, accuracy and 5-word practice). Feedback to the child is usually immediate, positive and limited to a few words. Teachers get the detailed miscue analysis.

### Cited Findings
- Amira listens to read-aloud, analyzes ORF in real time, and gives instant feedback. It uses AI for curriculum-based measurement and recommends resources — [FCRR](https://fcrr.org/node/7576); [TechLearning](https://www.techlearning.com/news/houghton-mifflin-harcourt-partners-with-ai-powered-reading-startup)
- Ello gives "support when your child needs help reading, gentle corrections when they make errors". The company stresses speech because young children cannot type — [Ello app listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1536720182); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ello-leverages-genai-launch-largest-131500632.html)
- Ello publishes no accuracy figures for its child speech model — [Ello app listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1536720182) (absence noted)
- Google Read Along's Diya detects mistakes and struggles, corrects them and praises successes. The Android app processes speech on-device and works offline after download. The 2022 web version processes speech locally in the browser — [Voicebot.ai](https://voicebot.ai/2020/05/08/google-launches-new-voice-assistant-and-app-to-teach-kids-to-read/); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2022/08/09/google-read-along-website/); [Hall County eLearning](https://elearning.hallco.org/google-read-along/)
- Google acknowledged that Diya uses general Google speech technology, which may not be ideal for children's speech — [Voicebot.ai](https://voicebot.ai/2020/05/08/google-launches-new-voice-assistant-and-app-to-teach-kids-to-read/)
- Microsoft Reading Coach gives the child a report with accuracy %, reading time, correct words per minute and the number of words to practice. A "Practice words" flow follows, with listening, syllable splitting and re-recording until the word turns green — [LCPS blog](https://blogs.lcps.org/inclusivedesign/tag/feedback); [Glow Scotland](https://blogs.glowscotland.org.uk/fa/digilearnfalk/2025/03/04/microsoft-reading-coach-get-started/)
- Stanford ROAR has a progress-monitoring study using Microsoft Reading Coach. Guidance is that students move on when they understand the passage and have corrected most mistakes, not when they reach 100% accuracy — [Stanford ROAR](https://roar.stanford.edu/progress-monitoring-study)

### Inferences
- The child-facing feedback pattern that recurs is: read the whole passage without interruption (or with light prompts), then celebrate, then practice a small number of words (about 5). This avoids constant interruption, which matters for dyslexic children whose reading is effortful.
- On-device ASR (Read Along) is a useful privacy model for school deployments with minors.

### Gaps
- No vendor publishes word-level recognition accuracy for children's speech or for dysfluent and dyslexic readers. False "error" flags are a known risk but are not quantified in the sources.

## Q4. Motivation mechanics, session length and avoiding frustration

### Takeaway
Rewards are mostly virtual currency, avatars and collectibles: Reading Eggs Golden Eggs and shop, Read Along stars and badges, Reading Coach badges and unlockable characters. Sessions are short, at about 5–7-minute units (Lexia) and about 10 minutes a day (Dyslexia Gold Engaging Eyes). Frustration is reduced with student choice of path, early test termination, manual placement override and limiting correction to a few words.

### Cited Findings
- Reading Eggs: Golden Eggs are awarded for achievements and spent in Reggie's Shop to decorate a house or dress an avatar. The Arcade unlocks at Lesson 7+ and My House at Lesson 11+ — [Sophie Online School guide](https://sophie.onlineschool.ca/books/learning-commons-for-families/page/reading-eggs-fast-phonics-reading-eggspress/export/html)
- Google Read Along: stars and badges — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/google-launches-a-new-read-along-app-to-help-you-with-homeschooling)
- Microsoft Reading Coach: an Achievements page with total time, sessions, average pronunciation accuracy and WPM, plus badges and unlockable characters and story settings. Reading Coach can generate AI stories where the child picks the character and setting — [LCPS blog](https://blogs.lcps.org/inclusivedesign/tag/reading-coach)
- Lexia Core5: students can see their own progress graph and choose their path — [Capterra review](https://www.capterra.com.au/software/210596/lexia-core5-reading); [Lexia llms-full.txt](https://www.lexialearning.com/llms-full.txt)
- Session length: Lexia units are about 5–7 minutes ([Bend-La Pine](https://blogs.bend.k12.or.us/instructionaltechnology/lexia-reading/)). Dyslexia Gold Engaging Eyes is about 10 minutes a day, while other vendor pages say 10–30 or 20 minutes 3–5 times a week ([Dyslexia Action Shop](https://dyslexiaactionshop.co.uk/dyslexia-gold-bundle-parents.html); [EdTech Impact](https://edtechimpact.com/products/dyslexia-gold/)). In the Nessy Godley study, children averaged 60 minutes a week ([Nessy Godley case study](https://nessy.com/en-us/nessy-use-at-godley-school-data-analysis-case-study/))
- Teach Your Monster to Read: the child customizes a monster and "teaches" it to read (the core narrative). Common Sense notes limited variety — [Common Sense Education](https://www.commonsense.org/education/app/teach-your-monster-to-read)
- Ello: the on-screen elephant buddy gives encouragement — [CO/AI listing](https://getcoai.com/tool-detail/ello/164021/)

### Inferences
- Short units (5–10 min), several per week, plus a persistent avatar or collection economy is the dominant engagement model. A narrative "teach the character" framing (Monster, Ello's elephant) puts the child in a helper role, which can lower anxiety for struggling readers.

### Gaps
- No source quantified how much rewards affect engagement or outcomes for dyslexic children specifically.

## Q5. Teacher and parent reporting

### Takeaway
Reports center on skills or levels mastered, time on task, accuracy, WCPM (for speech tools), and alerts or recommendations on who needs teacher-led instruction. Microsoft Insights adds aggregated "challenging words" and "challenging phonics rules" plus one-click targeted assignments. That closed loop from data to assignment is the most actionable pattern.

### Cited Findings
- Lexia myLexia: "Assessment Without Testing" reports progress by student, class, school and district without stopping instruction. It runs on web, iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch — [Lexia llms-full.txt](https://www.lexialearning.com/llms-full.txt)
- Lexia gives real-time progress monitoring and highlights where students need support, recommending Lexia Lessons and Skill Builders — [Lexia infographic](https://lexialearning.com/user_area/content_media/processed/2025-Q1-C5-With-What-Time-Infographic-v1-2-WEB.pdf)
- One reviewer criticized Lexia because only unit-level scores are visible, not individual task results — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com.au/software/210596/lexia-core5-reading)
- Microsoft Reading Progress Insights shows accuracy %, average words per minute, assignments completed, common challenging words and common challenging phonics rules. It can create a personalized "Challenge" assignment in one click — [Microsoft Education blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/education/blog/2023/02/enhance-literacy-development-with-reading-progress-and-coach/)
- Amira replaces hand-scoring of oral reading with instant data. Amira Instruct estimates the instructional time each student needs to reach mastery — [Amira research page](https://amiralearning.com/research)
- Learning Ally: book assignment plus tracking of pages and minutes read — [CEC partner page](https://2finfo.exceptionalchildren.org/partner-resources/products/learning-ally)
- Nessy: baseline from placement, then progress measured against it after each set of targets — [schoolreadinglist.co.uk](https://schoolreadinglist.co.uk/resources/nessy/)
- Amplio: background progress monitoring and documentation tools for MTSS/IEP — [Amplio home](https://ampliolearning.com/)

### Inferences
- Minimum viable dashboard for a Spanish planning tool: time on task, skills mastered, accuracy, WCPM (if speech is used), a list of persistent problem words or graphemes, and a "needs teacher attention" flag linked to a ready-to-use offline mini-lesson.

### Gaps
- No public information on exact alert thresholds (e.g., Lexia "Students of Concern" criteria) or on Ello parent report contents.

## Q6. Evidence and pricing (brief)

### Takeaway
Lexia Core5 has the strongest independent evidence (Evidence for ESSA "Strong", at least for struggling readers). Amira is "Promising" on Evidence for ESSA, although HMH markets it as strong. Nessy, Dyslexia Gold, Ello and Teach Your Monster have only vendor or anecdotal evidence. Pricing ranges from free (Khan Academy Kids, Teach Your Monster web, Google Read Along) to consumer subscriptions (Ello $9.99/month, Dyslexia Gold £18–24/month) to district licenses (Lexia, Amira).

### Cited Findings
- Lexia Core5 is rated "Strong" by Evidence for ESSA. Its RCT compared 65 users against 50 controls, and users were twice as likely to become proficient — [Lexia ESSA report](https://www.lexialearning.com/sites/default/files/Misc/Core5%20ESSA%20Strong%20Report%20-%20Impact%20of%20the%20Lexia%C2%AE%20Core5%C2%AE%20Reading%20Program%20on%20Students%20with%20Reading%20Difficulties.pdf). **Conflict**: a 2024 Lexia document says Strong applies to struggling readers and Promising to general elementary students — [Lexia ESSA evidence](https://www.lexialearning.com/research/essa-evidence)
- Amira is "Promising" on Evidence for ESSA (K–5). In a grades 1–4 study in an affluent, about 90% white district, ES = +0.64 on the WRMT Total Reading Composite — [FCRR](https://fcrr.org/node/7576). HMH claims "strong" — [HMH](https://www.hmhco.com/amira-access). Amira cites an ESSA Level II Louisiana study across 12 districts — [Amira research](https://amiralearning.com/research)
- Nessy: a Godley, Texas case study of 152 K–1 students reported about half a grade level of gain in 6–10 weeks, with no control group. A Northern Ireland comparison reported 73% higher ORF and 14.4% higher single-word reading after 6–9 weeks. Both are vendor-published. Nessy holds the BDA quality mark — [Nessy Godley](https://nessy.com/en-us/nessy-use-at-godley-school-data-analysis-case-study/); [Nessy NI study](https://nessy.com/en-us/the-effect-of-nessy-when-added-to-the-regular-curriculum/). An independent Southampton critique calls it promising, with limited long-term evidence — [Southampton EdPsych blog](https://blog.soton.ac.uk/edpsych/?p=1507)
- Dyslexia Gold: vendor trials in 2018–19, with only Engaging Eyes completed in 2019. The vendor reports gains in reading speed and reading age after 10+ plays — [The Dyslexia Shop](https://thedyslexiashop.co.uk/pages/dyslexia-gold-improve-your-childs-reading-and-spelling). Prices are £24/month (catch-up bundle) and £18/month (early-intervention bundle) — [Dyslexia Action Shop](https://dyslexiaactionshop.co.uk/dgrbsl1y-reading-bundle-dyslexia-gold.html)
- Ello: 7-day free trial, then $9.99/month (digital) — [Ello access](https://www.ello.com/access). Ello is named No. 5 on Fortune's 2024 Change the World list — [eSchool News](https://www.eschoolnews.com/newsline/2024/09/26/edtech-startup-ello-named-no-5-on-fortunes-change-the-world-list-2024/)
- Learning Ally claims it doubles the rate of reading growth in 50 days. I couldn't locate the underlying study — [Reading Rockets](https://readingrockets.org/resources/literacy-apps/learning-ally-audiobook-reader)
- Teach Your Monster to Read has no independent controlled trial. It is free on the web with a paid app — [Educational App Store](https://www.educationalappstore.com/app/teach-your-monster-to-read)
- Khan Academy Kids is fully free — [Snappit](https://www.snappit.app/blog/best-reading-apps-for-kids)

### Gaps
- Current 2026 prices for Lexia, Amira, Reading Eggs, Epic, Hooked on Phonics and Nessy were not retrieved.
- I didn't check What Works Clearinghouse entries.

## Q7. Transfer to a Spanish (transparent orthography) context

### Takeaway
The architecture transfers well: placement, then short skill units, then decodable text, then oral reading with speech recognition, then a teacher dashboard. The content emphasis must change. Spanish grapheme-phoneme mapping is nearly one-to-one, so children decode accurately early. Dyslexia in Spanish shows up mainly as slow, effortful reading rather than inaccurate reading. Fluency (speed and automaticity) and syllable-based work should get more weight than English-style irregular-word and long phonics sequences. Amplio (Esperanza) and Amira already offer Spanish.

### Cited Findings
- Amplio has a Spanish structured-literacy dyslexia curriculum, Esperanza, alongside the English MTA — [Amplio home](https://ampliolearning.com/)
- Amira tutors and listens in English and Spanish. A DePaul study on bilingual literacy is listed — [Amira research](https://amiralearning.com/research)
- Khan Academy Kids has limited Spanish content — [Snappit](https://www.snappit.app/blog/best-reading-apps-for-kids)
- Microsoft Reading Coach / Reading Progress compute WCPM and accuracy and split words into syllables. These mechanics are not English-specific — [LCPS blog](https://blogs.lcps.org/inclusivedesign/tag/reading-coach)

### Inferences
(These are the researcher's reasoning from the general dyslexia literature. They were not fetched in this session, so they need a citation from another researcher before being stated as fact.)
- **Keep:** placement with no supports; short 5–10 min units; escalating error scaffolds (retry, hint, re-teach, teacher flag); 5-word post-reading practice; WCPM, accuracy, time and problem-grapheme dashboards; avatar/currency rewards; read-aloud TTS with synchronized highlighting (Learning Ally, Khan Kids) for access to grade-level text.
- **Change:** the phonics scope can be much shorter (about 27 letters and a few digraphs: ch, ll, rr, qu, gu/güe, c/z, g/j), so the emphasis should move to syllable-level work (CV, CVC, inverse VC, trabadas such as "bl", "tr"), automaticity drills with timed syllable and pseudoword reading, and repeated reading of decodable passages with WCPM goals. Spanish norms for words per minute must replace English ones.
- **Less relevant:** English "tricky word"/sight-word modules and rule-heavy spelling mnemonics (Nessy-style). Spanish spelling difficulty concentrates on b/v, g/j, h, ll/y and c/s/z, which are spelling-only ambiguities. Reading is still regular.
- **ASR caveat:** Spanish child speech models need dialect coverage (seseo, aspiration of /s/, yeísmo) to avoid falsely flagging regional pronunciations as errors.
- **Eye-tracking training** (Dyslexia Gold Engaging Eyes) has a weak evidence base and should not be prioritized.
